Where the time goes

How Argon runs out of time: 37 of its 120 runs hit the 80 minute limit; none of Claude's or GPT's 360 did.

At 80 minutes

  • The 37 sit on 26 of 40 tasks and average 0.02. Argon's other 83 runs average 0.86.
  • Tool waits are about 4% of those runs; the rest is the model generating. Provider speed cannot be separated from model behaviour: there are no provider-side logs.
  • Per output token Argon is no slower than the others.
  • Very long single turns: median 727 output tokens per turn against 363 in finished runs and 417 to 491 for the other models. 11.3% of calls produce 8,000 tokens or more, under 0.1% for the others.
  • Re-checking: in 30 of 120 Argon runs, 1 turn in 5 or more opens with a re-check phrase such as "wait" or "hold on" (a word-pattern count); 0 of 360 runs for the others.

Rerun at 5 hours

    Better and worse, side by side

    Argon is a careful reader and a poor finisher.

    Better when the answer is in the written rules and the data: it holds to the documented rule, scans whole tables instead of samples, and checks event order before trusting a status. Worse when it has to decide or wrap up: it improvises its own version of a definition where the documentation leaves room, keeps re-checking after the figures are settled, and fumbles delivery (scripts re-run so a write goes out twice, a final message without the answer).

    Method

    Same harness, same tasks, disclosed end to end.

    Denominators

    Run policy

    • Terminus 2 agent for all four models, reasoning effort high, internet on, web search off.
    • Counted runs: first 3 finished runs per task and model; 160 of 160 cells have 3.
    • Full marks: every check passed. Mean score: weighted share of checks passed.
    • 80 minute view: one 4,800 second agent limit for every model.
    • 5 hour view: only the 37 Argon runs that hit the limit were rerun, at 18,000 seconds. All four models are scored on the same tasks: a task whose Argon rerun had not finished is dropped for everyone.
    • Excluded everywhere: 3 proof runs and 229 runs of a stopped wave.

    What this does not establish

    • Three runs per task is not a stable rate for one task.
    • Only high effort was run. Provider latency cannot be separated from model behaviour.
    • The dropped tasks are where Argon took longest, so the 5 hour task set favours Argon; the sensitivity line keeps all 40.
    • Failure modes and side-by-side notes are one reader's judgement per run, checked against the grader's reasons.

    Provenance

    • Run records, timestamps, token counts and grader output pulled read-only from the evaluation platform on 2026-10-15.
    • Tasks appear by alias (T01 to T40) and a generic title. No task ids, expected values or names from task data.
